Roadmap

  1. Phase 1 · pilot (0 to 1 year)A buffer at an existing municipal parking area, shared by one shipper and one carrier; simple call-in by phone or app, linked to PeriPass where possible; staggered departures with Steenbergen; count traffic and run the simulation again. Lead: OPBR with the municipality, Refresco and Steenbergen.
  2. Phase 2 · scale up (1 to 3 years)More buffer locations, such as the mobility hub location and Rijnhoek 2; connect the yard systems of more companies.
  3. Phase 3 · create space (3+ years)A parking roof or deck for staff cars to free ground-level space for truck buffers.

How this works
  • The model simulates one working day minute by minute and repeats it for 30 days with different random truck arrivals (report, chapter 7). Every minute it compares the traffic at the Botrotonde with its capacity; a truck counts as two cars. Vehicles that cannot pass form a queue; congestion means a delay of 2 minutes or more.
  • Traffic has three parts: trucks of the four surveyed companies (STEF 150, Steenbergen 180, anonymous 80 and Refresco 50 a day), about 1,500 employee cars, and 6,600 through trips between the N11 and the A12 (Movares, 2024, in Hogeschool Utrecht, 2025).
  • Refresco and STEF each have 6 docks (60 and 75 minutes per truck on average). Steenbergen has 65 trucks, 50 spaces on site and 22 rented spaces; 95% leave between 07:00 and 07:15. A delivery truck waiting on the road lowers the capacity by 5%, up to 30%.
  • Option C: delivery trucks wait in a 16-space buffer on the municipal truck parking on the Europaweg and are called in about 3 minutes before a dock is free; carriers park there from 17:00. Option D: Steenbergen spreads its departure over 60 minutes.
  • Limitations (report, 7.6): employee cars, road capacity and the effect of a waiting truck are estimates; truck numbers are self-reported; only the four responding companies are modelled. Roads and buildings: OpenStreetMap. STEF lies outside this part of the map. The carrier trucks parked on the street at night are those of the anonymous carrier; Steenbergen's rented spaces cover its own fleet.
